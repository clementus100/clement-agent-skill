# Playbook: Yoruba historical films (AFONJA, Ogunmola)

**Use when:** Clement wants a new part, scene, voice line, subtitle or promo for his Yoruba history series.

## Workflow
1. Script: write in English, then Yoruba dialogue (proofread diacritics), then subtitles. Check history before it goes in the script; mark dramatised dialogue.
2. Voices: use the locked cast voices (see afonja.md). Never swap a voice mid-series.
3. Stills: text-to-image only for new faces. Keep each character's look stable across shots.
4. Clips: image-to-video, 5 to 10 s per shot. Locked-off steady camera, faces toward camera, unobstructed. Each person moves independently with varied natural gestures; no synchronized or choreographed motion.
5. Lip-sync every spoken line. If a line cannot sync, put it over a wide, back or reaction shot.
6. Edit as ONE film: crossfades, a continuous underlay sound bed (wind, fire, crowd murmur, drums building through battles, all synthesized), English subtitles, end card.
7. Export 1080p master and an upscaled 4K file for the YouTube channel; deliver large files by download link.

## Quality bar
- Not congested: individual warriors, no crowd blur, no smoke across faces.
- Real motion in every shot; no Ken Burns; no reversed clips.
- Re-render a flagged shot at most once; disclose anything left unfixed.

## Pitfalls seen
- Similar costumes make two characters look alike (Solagberu vs fighters in cream turbans).
- Red caps or red-clad extras can look like Afonja.
- Provider queues stall; render budgets have a rolling 24 h cap: batch by whole scenes and stop cleanly on a budget error.
